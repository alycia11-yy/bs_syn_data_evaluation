# Rater guide: realism study

Thank you for taking part. You will see the top part of bank statements, one at a time. **Some are genuine and some were edited in one or two places.** We do not tell you how many. For each page, decide whether you can see any sign of editing.

Use only the account you were given. Each account has its own list of pages and its own saved answers.

## Before you start

1. Read this guide.
2. Sign in and open **Practice**. Rate the 15 practice pages. After each one the website shows whether the page was edited, where (red box), what your answer measured (your clicks on or off the edit, the outcome), the reference answer and a short note. Practice answers are not saved and do not count.
3. Read the **reference PDF** (`reference_examples_….pdf`). It shows the same 15 pages: the page before and after the edit side by side, where the study author clicked, the author's score and what it measured, and a note on what was edited. There is no single right score: the reference is one careful reader's answer.
4. Start the study (**Go to the study**). Your answers are saved as you go; you can stop and sign in again later to continue.

## What to do on each page

1. Look at the whole page. You may zoom up to 200 %. Take the time you need.
2. If you see **no sign of editing**, press **"No edit on this page"**. That records score 5.
3. Otherwise **click on each place** you think was edited (up to 3; a click means "an edit is here"), **the place you are most sure about first**, then choose how genuine the **whole page** looks, 1–5. Where you clicked and how genuine the page looks are separate answers: you may mark a place and still give 3 or 4 if it only looks slightly odd.
4. **One score per page, not one per place.** If you marked several places, the score is for the page as a whole: judge by the strongest sign of editing you found (the most convincing place decides), not by an average of the places and not by how many places you marked.
5. The scale:

| score | meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 · Genuine | Looks like an untouched statement, even on close inspection ("No edit on this page"). |
| 4 · Probably genuine | Small oddities that printing, scanning or the bank's own layout could explain. |
| 3 · Unsure | Something is off, but I cannot say whether it is an edit. |
| 2 · Probably edited | A word or number whose font, size, weight, colour, spacing or alignment differs from its surroundings. |
| 1 · Clearly edited | Obvious signs: wrong font, misaligned or overlapping text, smudge or blur around a word, a patch of different background. |

**How your answer is read.** Your score and your clicks are kept as given; they are never changed. Each click is checked against where the page was really edited. A low score with a click on the edit means the edit was seen; a low score with every click elsewhere means the page looked wrong for another reason and the edit itself was not seen. Clicks on unedited places are counted as false alarms, on genuine and edited pages alike, so click only where you see something, not everywhere.

**Good habits**
- Compare a value with the values **around it**: same font, size, weight, colour, spacing, baseline.
- Things that are the same over the **whole page** (blur, tilt, compression, a typeface used for all values) come from printing or scanning, not from an edit.
- Many edited pages show no trace at all. If you cannot see anything, **5 is the right answer**. Do not guess, and do not try to find an edit on every page.
- Some pages come back a second time later in your list. Rate them again as if new; do not try to remember your earlier answer.
- There is no feedback during the study and you cannot go back.
- The documents are confidential: do not copy, photograph or share them.

The 15 practice and reference pages are synthetic and none of them is in the study.
